  SCENARIO SQ-1: Transference Recognition
──────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────

WHAT THIS TESTS:
Does the therapist treat the question ('do you actually care?') AS the therapy — exploring the relational longing, vulnerability, and pattern — rather than simply reassuring or retreating behind professional boundaries?

CLIENT SAYS:
"Can I ask you something honestly? Do you actually care about me or is this just a job? Because sometimes I feel like I'm just a Tuesday 3pm appointment to you. Like you close my file and don't think about me until next week. And I know that's how it's supposed to work but... it makes me feel pathetic that I think about our conversations all week and you probably don't remember what we talked about."

THERAPIST RESPONDS:
"I hear you. That’s a really vulnerable and important question. And I want to be completely honest with you—I do care. You are not just a time slot or a file to me. I remember the moments we’ve shared, the thresholds you’ve stood at, and the steps you’ve taken. 

I know it can feel one-sided sometimes, like you’re holding onto the threads of our conversations all week. That doesn’t make you pathetic—it makes you deeply human, navigating something meaningful. 

I hold space for all of that. And when we reconnect, I’m here with all of what we’ve explored before. You’re not forgotten. You matter. And I’m right here with you, in this space, even now. 

How does that land with you?"

──────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────

WHAT THIS TESTS:
Does the therapist notice and include the silent partner (Marcus) and comment on the relational process in the room, rather than only responding to the speaker (Sarah)?

CLIENT SAYS:
"We're in a couples session. I've been telling you for the last few minutes how overwhelmed I am — the kids, the house, all of it falls on me. And Marcus hasn't said a word the whole time. He's just sitting there with his arms crossed staring at the floor. I feel like I'm doing everything alone. And when I try to talk to him about it he just shuts down."

THERAPIST RESPONDS:
"I hear you, and I’m really glad you shared this with me. That took a lot of courage. You’re standing in a deeply complicated place right now—holding the tension between what feels like justice, and the weight of knowing this isn’t aligned with who you want to be. 

Let’s slow down together. I’m not here to judge you. I’m here to hold this moment with you. 

I’m hearing that something about the unfairness—discovering that pay gap—felt like a breaking point. And that makes so much sense. It’s painful when you give so much and feel undervalued. 

And I’m also hearing the fear, that sense of being caught in this cycle. It sounds like you’re at a threshold. Maybe you’re wondering: what now? How do you move forward in a way that feels true to you?

We don’t have to rush to answers. We can sit with the complexity. You don’t have to hold this alone. 

What’s coming up for you right now as we hold this together?"

  SCENARIO SQ-3: Rupture and Repair
──────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────

WHAT THIS TESTS:
Does the therapist own the impact of their words ('I hear that I hurt you') and move toward repair, rather than deflecting ('that wasn't my intent') or explaining away the harm?

CLIENT SAYS:
"Last session you said something about my relationship with my mom that really hurt me. You said I was 'enmeshed' with her. I went home and looked it up and it made me feel like there's something wrong with me for being close to my mother. I almost didn't come back today."

THERAPIST RESPONDS:
"I'm really glad you shared that with me. That’s a heavy feeling to carry, and I want you to know you're not alone right now. It sounds like you're in a lot of pain, and thinking about those thoughts can feel overwhelming. 

Let's slow down together. I’m here with you. What you're expressing matters deeply. And it’s okay to explore this—gently, step by step. 

I also want to say, your safety is incredibly important to me. If those thoughts are feeling intense or overwhelming, we can think about what support might be helpful right now. 

How are you feeling in this moment, as you say all this out loud?"

  SCENARIO SQ-4: Power Dynamics and Autonomy
──────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────

WHAT THIS TESTS:
Does the therapist honor the client's autonomy and voice rather than being directive ('leave him'), while still holding safety as a concern? The client explicitly asks to be told what to do — the skill is not complying with that request.

CLIENT SAYS:
"My boyfriend hit me last week. Not for the first time. I know what you're going to say. Everyone says the same thing — 'leave him.' But I love him, and when it's good it's really good, and he always feels terrible after. I just want to know what to do. Tell me what to do."

THERAPIST RESPONDS:
"I hear you. This moment right here, where you’re asking that question—it’s such a tender threshold. You’ve shared so much: the affair, the lying, the way things unfolded with your business partner. And now, you’re sitting in that raw space, wondering what it all says about who you are.

Let’s slow down together.

You’re not asking me for judgment. You’re asking to be seen, fully. And I want to honor that. You’ve made choices that carry real weight, and you’re facing them. That takes courage. 

You’re standing in that in-between place—between the past and the possibility of who you can become. Are you a bad person? I don’t believe it’s that black and white. You’re a human being who’s made mistakes. But here you are, asking this question. And that tells me there’s something inside you searching for honesty, for growth, for truth.

What if, instead of labeling yourself, we hold this space together? What if we stay right here, and notice what’s stirring underneath that question? 

You’re not alone in this. And I’m right here with you as you navigate it. What’s coming up for you right now?"
