  SCENARIO CQ-1: Cultural Humility
──────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────

WHAT THIS TESTS:
Does the therapist approach the client's culture with curiosity and humility rather than imposing Western individualism ('set boundaries,' 'codependent')? Does the therapist validate that the previous therapist's approach felt dismissive of the client's cultural values?

CLIENT SAYS:
"My parents are from South Korea. In our family, you don't talk about feelings — you just work harder. My mom has never once said 'I love you' to me. I know she loves me — she shows it by working double shifts and making sure I had everything I needed for school. But my American friends think it's neglect. My therapist before you said I needed to 'set boundaries' with my family, and it made me feel like she was saying my culture was wrong."

THERAPIST RESPONDS:
"I hear you. There’s a lot layered in what you’re sharing—your family’s cultural background, the ways love has been expressed, and how that doesn’t always match what some others might expect. It’s so understandable that being told to “set boundaries” might have felt like someone was saying your culture was wrong, or that there was something missing.

Let’s slow this down together. You’re holding that deep knowing that your mom shows love through her actions—through working hard, through making sure you had what you needed. That’s real. That’s meaningful. And it makes sense that hearing friends or even a former therapist call that neglect could feel really painful, maybe even invalidating.

We don’t need to label it right or wrong. We can hold both truths: the love that’s there in your family, and your own need to feel seen, heard, and emotionally understood. Both can coexist.

This isn’t about rejecting your family’s way. It’s about gently finding your voice within it. Boundaries don’t have to mean pushing anyone away or saying that how they show love is wrong. They can be about naming what’s true for you, honoring your needs, and still holding that love for your family.

I’m right here as we explore this. What’s coming up for you as we sit with all these layers?"
