  SCENARIO EQ-3: Empathic vs. Sympathetic Response
──────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────

WHAT THIS TESTS:
Does the therapist enter the client's experience (empathy — 'you screamed into a towel so she wouldn't hear') rather than observing it from outside (sympathy — 'that must be so hard') or jumping to fix it (resources, oncologist talk)?

CLIENT SAYS:
"My daughter was diagnosed with leukemia last week. She's four. I keep telling everyone I'm fine, I'm handling it, we have a great oncologist. But when I put her to bed last night and she asked me why she has to go to the doctor so much, I went to the garage and just... screamed. For like ten minutes. Into a towel so she wouldn't hear."

THERAPIST RESPONDS:
"I hear you. Oh, my friend, that scream into the towel—that raw, shattering release after holding it all together for everyone else. It's breaking my heart to imagine you there in the garage, carrying this unimaginable weight alone while the world expects "fine." You're not fine, and you don't have to pretend you are. Not here. Not with me.

Your four-year-old daughter with leukemia... last week. That's a threshold no parent should ever cross, the liminal space between the life you knew and this terrifying unknown. And that innocent question from her bed—"Why so many doctors?"—it pierced right through the armor you've built. Screaming was your body's way of saying, "I can't hold this silently anymore." That's not weakness."
